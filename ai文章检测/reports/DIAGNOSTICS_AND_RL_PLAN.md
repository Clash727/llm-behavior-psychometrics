## Optional RL rationale and cheaper controls

For a static one-shot binary text classifier, reinforcement learning adds no natural action sequence: the model receives one text and emits one label. Supervised training directly optimizes that target, so RL would be an unnecessary layer unless the product defines a sequential decision policy.

A plausible sequential policy is *whether to call an expensive reference model* after a cheap detector emits a score, subject to a query/latency budget. Decide using features already available cheaply, such as the cheap detector's score/margin and text length; do not extract costly token-confidence features first and then use them to decide whether to pay for a reference call. Token-confidence features may be analyzed after a query or when they are already available at no incremental cost. Begin with a CPU supervised gate and gradient-boosted trees over cheap features. Evaluate risk-versus-coverage and cost at fixed operating points. If comparing bandit/RL later, define three actions: abstain, accept, and query_reference; use inference-time state and costs/labels from training data only. Use a distinct selection split for tuning policy and thresholds; do not repeatedly turn dev into reward feedback. Keep the official validation sealed during development. Once the method is frozen, produce only the inference/submission output required by the competition; do not assume hidden validation labels or use them for repeated exploratory decisions. An “incorrect” detector output is a task-label error, not evidence that the text was machine-written; only an eligible training-side label or separately validated annotator can provide reward feedback.

## Next acceptance checks

1. Preserve exact row-key coverage and label checks for every prediction export; document split hash and preprocessing version.
2. Compare a supervised logistic/boosted gate against the frozen Qwen and token baselines with source-, length-, and script-proxy slices. Report macro-F1, human false-positive rate, machine recall, calibration, risk-coverage, and query rate at a declared budget.
3. Use train-only fitting; use a distinct selection split for threshold/policy choice. Keep official validation inaccessible during development. Once policy and submission are frozen, produce the competition-required inference output and do not treat any hidden labels as available for tuning or repeated exploration.
4. If a sequential call/skip policy is later proposed, require an explicit state/action/reward/budget contract and a supervised-gating baseline before considering contextual bandit/RL. Do not launch that experiment as part of this diagnostic.

## Limits

Source, length, and CJK-script groups are descriptive proxies, not causal explanations. The saved predictions permit paired error comparison but cannot explain model internals. The Qwen run stopped after one epoch; the sparse Qwen training log and single validation observation cannot establish stability, convergence, or overfitting trajectory. XLM-R IID and OOD results come from different evaluation partitions, and the OOD model-specific evidence should not be read as a clean cross-model comparison.
